For the workshop

Built for the garage where everyone keeps working.

Five mechanics, two ramps, a front desk that also orders the parts and an owner who still turns a spanner. That is the workshop this is meant for. Not the franchised dealership with a call centre behind it.

Book a demo → Work it out first
This fits
  • Independent and all-makes garages
  • Workshops with two to fifteen mechanics
  • MOT-approved businesses
  • Garages where the front desk also does a hundred other things
  • Businesses that already have enough customers, but too little time
This does not fit
  • Franchised dealers with their own contact centre and fixed scripts
  • Chains that want to steer everything centrally from one system
  • Businesses that only sell and have no workshop
  • Anyone looking for a free chatbot for the website

If you are in this list, we would rather say so in the first conversation than in month three.

A Tuesday

This is what an ordinary day looks like.

On the left what happens. On the right what becomes of it without a system.

07:40

Someone calls before the doors open

A customer with a flat tyre tries before driving to work. The phone is still on the answering machine.

Without

He calls the next garage on the list and you never hear about it.

09:15

Three calls at once

The front desk has someone at the counter, the second line is ringing and a supplier is waiting with a delivery note.

Without

Two out of three callers get an engaged tone. Nobody knows how many there were.

11:30

A quote for a timing belt goes out

Eight hundred euros, neatly drawn up, sent by email. The customer says he will think about it.

Without

Nobody follows up. In three weeks nobody remembers that quote existed.

14:00

A no-show on ramp two

The two o'clock appointment does not turn up. There is no time left to fill the gap.

Without

An hour and a half of workshop stands still while the costs simply carry on.

17:50

Forty MOTs expiring next month

They are in your system. Someone ought to send letters or messages about it.

Without

It goes on the list for next week. That list has been getting pushed forward for two years.

20:30

A message through the website

Someone looks for a garage in the evening for the major service on his Passat and fills in the form.

Without

It comes in at half past eight tomorrow morning. By 20:45 he has already booked elsewhere.

Six moments on one Tuesday. None of them is about having too few customers, and none of them is solved by working harder.

Where you start

Not everything at once. The biggest gap first.

In almost every workshop the fastest gain sits in the same three places. That is where we switch it on first, the rest comes later.

First

Missed calls

This is the only gap you already know exists and whose size you do not know. Measurable immediately, closable immediately.

 Then

MOT reminder

You already have the list. The only thing missing is someone who works through it every week, with a date attached instead of a request.

 Dan

Quote follow-up

The work is already done: the price has been calculated and sent. Only the final nudge is missing, and that is exactly where the margin sits.

Then come the other seven. See them all

Automotive knowledge

It knows the difference between an inspection and a clutch.

A general assistant asks general questions. This system knows an MOT is an hour and a half and a timing belt is a day.

Registration as the starting point

Seven characters and the conversation is no longer about which car it is, but about when it can come. Make, model, year and MOT expiry date are already there.

Jobs have a duration

A tyre change, a service, an inspection and a clutch each need a different slot. An appointment that does not fit your planning is no help.

Parts and courtesy car

The two questions that delay every appointment. They are asked and answered in the same conversation, instead of over three call-backs.

Seasons

Twice a year a tyre change, in autumn the winter service. They are the most predictable weeks of your year, and usually the worst prepared.

Dutch and French

The customer's language is recognised and the conversation carries on in that language. For anyone near the language border, that saves half a front desk.

Your tone, not ours

You read the messages before they go live and adjust them. A village garage sounds different from a business on an industrial estate, and so it should.

Limits

What this does not do.

Every supplier has a list of capabilities. This list is more useful, because it decides whether you are disappointed after three months.

  • No diagnosisThe system does not diagnose a fault and gives no technical advice. That stays with your mechanic.
  • No prices out of thin airIt only names amounts you have set. For the rest, a person gets involved.
  • No customer acquisitionThis does not conjure up new customers. It makes sure you lose fewer of them.
  • No replacement for your front deskIt takes over the repetitive work. The conversations that matter still end up with a person.
  • Not an IT projectNo migration, no months-long rollout. If it is not working after a few weeks, you switch it off.
  • No promised figuresWe do not yet have a measured result in a workshop. What we are doing about that is on the numbers page.

One Tuesday. Everything that came past.

We take twenty minutes and walk through your Tuesday. What came in, what happened to it and where it got stuck. After that you know whether this is for you.
