Design

What the integration is going to do.

Recognising the vehicle

Make, model, year and fuel. The customer does not have to explain what he drives and you do not have to guess.

MOT expiry date

The trigger for the entire reminder workflow, and at the same time the answer to whether an inspection is still due.

The right job and duration

Which car it is helps determine how long a service takes and which parts go with it.

Asking fewer questions

Every question you do not have to ask is one less moment at which a customer drops out.

State of play in development

This is being worked on. In the conversations you see today we already use the registration as the starting point of the conversation; the direct integration with the register is not finished. As long as that is the case, this says "in development".

What is already possible today
  • The registration as the starting point of the conversation
  • Vehicle data you supply or record yourself
  • MOT dates from your own records
What is not yet possible
  • Querying the open vehicle register directly
  • Automatic updating when an MOT has been registered
  • Data that goes beyond what is publicly available

Belgian number plates work differently from Dutch ones. If you work on both sides of the border, say so in the first conversation.

Questions we get

Short and honest.

Do you store registrations?

Only for as long as the conversation and the appointment need it, with a retention period you set yourself. What is stored is in the privacy policy.

Is that simply allowed?

Vehicle data by registration is publicly available. What we store and link to a person does fall under the GDPR, and we handle that the same way as the rest of your customer data.

Does this also work for Belgian plates?

The Dutch register is a different source from the Belgian one. For Belgian businesses we work today with the data you supply yourself.

What if the registration is wrong?

Then it asks further instead of guessing. A typo should not produce the wrong appointment.
