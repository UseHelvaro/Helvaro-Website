Home · Agents · Tyre change
Agent 09 of ten

Two weeks a year you cannot keep up. The rest of the year the storage is full.

Everyone calls in the same week, and everyone wants Saturday. Whoever approaches their tyre customers three weeks earlier spreads that same turnover across a month and keeps time free for the work that earns more.

Book a demo → This is how the system fits together
Without this

The most predictable peak of your year, and every year you are unprepared for it.

In October and in April the same thing happens. The first night frost or the first warm week, and then everyone calls at once. Your front desk is packed, the phone does not stop, and you have to send people away to two weeks' time.

Meanwhile your storage is full of tyres belonging to customers whose contact details you have. The only thing missing is someone to approach them one by one from mid-September.

A tyre change is not much turnover in itself. But it is the moment you see the car, measure the tread depth and sell the next set.

  • Two weeks of chaos instead of four weeks of spread-out work.
  • Customers you have to turn away go to the fast-fit centre round the corner.
  • Tyres that have been sitting in your storage for two seasons, belonging to customers who no longer come.
The workflow

What the system does.

The same turnover, spread over a month instead of two weeks.

  1. 01

    Your tyre records as a list

    Everyone whose tyres you have in storage comes into view. That is a list you already have and that usually does nothing.

  2. 02

    Approaching before the peak begins

    From mid-September and mid-March messages go out, spread across the weeks. You decide how many per day, so your planning can handle it.

  3. 03

    Offering a date, not asking for a call

    Two concrete moments in the message, one of which is on a weekday. That takes pressure off Saturday.

  4. 04

    Tread and condition included

    If you know a set is nearly worn out, that is in the message. That is the natural moment to talk about new tyres.

  5. 05

    Clearing out storage

    Customers whose tyres have been sitting for two seasons are asked whether they still want them. That saves space and argument.

  6. 06

    The peak spread out

    You see how many changes are booked per week, so you spot in time whether it is going off balance.

This is how it sounds

A conversation that comes out of this.

An example, not a recording of a customer. You set the tone yourself, this one is ours.

WhatsApp · mid-SeptemberExample
HelvaroHello Ruud, your winter tyres are in storage with us. Shall we book the change before it gets busy? Tuesday 8 October at 14:00 or Saturday 12 October at 09:00.
CustomerIs it necessary yet? It is still warm.
HelvaroNot yet, but from the end of October we fill up and then Saturdays are often booked three weeks ahead. Booking now costs you nothing and you get the date you want.
CustomerLet us do the Tuesday. And how are my summer tyres doing?
HelvaroAt the last change there was 3.5 mm on the front tyres. They will probably last one more summer, but not two. We will measure them again and let you know.
Booked, before the peak Tuesday 8 October · 14:00 · tyre change · 45 min
What it produces

The same customers, less chaos.

The peak becomes a period

Four weeks of spread-out work instead of two weeks in which you have to turn people away.

Filling weekdays

By offering weekday slots first, you take pressure off Saturday.

Selling tyres at the right moment

The change is the only moment you see the tread depth. Having that conversation is easier if it was already in the message.

Your storage empties out

Sets belonging to customers who no longer come are collected or disposed of. That frees up space you need.

In the system

Not a loose little helper.

This is a seasonal workflow that switches on twice a year. Because it sits in the same system, it knows who has already been in for something else in the meantime.

Which layers this touches
  • UnderstandWhich customer, which set, what was measured at the last change.
  • ActApproach, offer a date and lock it in.
  • ConnectThe changes end up spread across your planning.
  • MeasureHow many approached, how many booked, how the weeks are spread.
Works together with
  • Workshop schedulingMakes sure the changes do not all fall on the same morning.
  • ServiceCombines the change with a service if that is nearly due anyway.
  • No-showsConfirms and reminds, because in a busy week a gap is extra expensive.
Limits

What this agent does not do.

  • He does not sell tyres and names no prices you have not set.
  • He does not judge whether a tyre is still good. Your mechanic measures that.
  • He does not throw away stored tyres. He only asks about them.
  • Without a record of who has which tyres in storage, there is little to approach.

Start in September. Not in the week of the first frost.
