The workflows

Ten agents. One system underneath.

This is not a shop with ten separate products. These are ten workflows within the same system: they share the same memory, write into the same planning and add up in the same overview. You switch on what you need and leave the rest off.

Book a demo → This is how the system fits together
Why this hangs together

Ten loose tools never become a process.

You can find a separate supplier for each of these tasks. Then you have ten subscriptions, ten inboxes and zero overview.

One memory

A correction you make once then applies to every agent. The MOT agent knows that the same customer called yesterday about his brakes.

One planning

Everything comes out at the same slot in the same calendar. No two agents promising the same morning to two people.

One overview

What came in, what came out and what is still open sits on one screen. Not spread over ten dashboards.

One set of rules

What an agent may do on its own and what goes past you first, you set once. That then applies to everything that is running.

Advice

Start with one.

Switching everything on at once sounds efficient and rarely works. You then do not know what is working, your team loses the overview, and the first mistake in one workflow puts your whole business on edge. Switch one on, measure for four weeks, then the next.

  • Do you not know where to startThen it is missed calls, because there you immediately see how big the problem is.
  • Is your workshop quietThen MOT reminders, because that list is already there and it fills your empty weeks.
  • Do a lot of quotes go outThen quote follow-up, because that is where the margin you have already paid for sits.
