Home · Agents · Quote follow-up
Agent 08 of ten

The price has been calculated and sent. After that, nothing happens any more.

The expensive work is already done: looking things up, requesting parts, putting together an amount. What is missing is the final nudge, and that is exactly where the margin sits. A quote without follow-up is an investment you throw away.

Book a demo → This is how the system fits together
Without this

No yes, no no, just silence.

A customer gets a price for a timing belt or a set of shock absorbers and says he will think about it. That is not a rejection, that is a customer who does not want to decide just yet. If nobody comes back to it afterwards, that hesitation turns into a no all by itself.

Following up almost never happens. Not out of unwillingness, but because there is always something more urgent than someone who is not getting in touch. And because calling about money feels awkward.

A no is valuable too, by the way. Then you know you do not have to schedule that car and the lingering stops.

  • The research work has already been paid for, but yields nothing.
  • The customer drives on with a problem and comes back broken down at someone else.
  • Nobody knows which quotes are still open and which are dead.
The workflow

What the system does.

Three contact moments, in your tone, and then clarity.

  1. 01

    The quote comes into view

    As soon as a price goes out, it is on the list with a date. You see in one screen what is open and for how long.

  2. 02

    After two days: is it clear

    No sales pitch, but the question of whether the quote arrived and whether anything was unclear. That is usually the real objection.

  3. 03

    Questions get answered

    Why is it so expensive, does it have to be now, can it be done in stages, how long can I keep driving. Those questions are the reason it went quiet.

  4. 04

    After a week: offer a date

    Not asking again whether he has thought about it, but naming a concrete slot. That makes yes easier than no.

  5. 05

    After two weeks: close it off neatly

    A final message saying the quote is about to expire and that he can still respond. After that it really stops.

  6. 06

    Every quote gets a status

    Booked, declined or expired. No more list of things nobody knows are still alive.

This is how it sounds

A conversation that comes out of this.

An example, not a recording of a customer. You set the tone yourself, this one is ours.

WhatsApp · seven days after the quoteExample
HelvaroHello Peter, last week we sent you the price for the timing belt on your Passat: €780 including water pump and labour. Shall I keep a day free for it?
CustomerThat is quite a lot of money. Can it wait a bit?
HelvaroAt 168,000 km you are at the interval where it should be replaced. You can keep driving, but if the belt snaps the engine damage is many times this amount. We would rather book it in than have you run that risk.
CustomerAnd can I borrow a car in the meantime?
HelvaroYes, we have a courtesy car available, I will reserve that as well. We can start on Thursday the 21st.
CustomerAll right, go ahead.
Quote accepted Thursday 21 · timing belt · €780 · courtesy car included
What it produces

The margin that was already in the building.

Work where the costs are already in

The looking up and the calculating has been done and paid for. Every quote that still turns into a yes is almost entirely margin.

Objections come to the surface

Too expensive is rarely the real objection. Usually it is uncertainty about urgency or about transport. Both can be answered.

A no is a gain too

Then you know that car is not coming back and you stop counting on it in your planning.

Nobody has to call about money

The most uncomfortable phone call of the week disappears from your front desk's to-do list.

In the system

Not a loose little helper.

A follow-up email from a package does not know what happened in the workshop. This agent knows the car, the mileage and the conversation the quote came out of.

Limits

What this agent does not do.

  • He does not negotiate the price and gives no discount.
  • He does not change the quote. If something has to change, it goes to your front desk.
  • He gives no technical advice on whether something can wait, only what you have recorded.
  • He stops after three messages, even if nothing has come back.
