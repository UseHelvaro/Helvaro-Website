The distinction

Three things that often look the same.

Chatbot

Answers questions.

It sits on your site, knows your opening hours and refers on. When the conversation is over, nothing has changed in your planning.

Single agent

Performs one task.

Sends MOT reminders, for example. Handy, but it knows nothing of yesterday's conversation and writes nowhere.

The Helvaro system

Captures, understands, acts, connects and measures.

The agents share the same memory, write into the same planning and add up in the same overview. That is what makes it a process and not a loose little helper.

Five layers

Where a chatbot stops, the rest begins.

You can buy the first three layers from dozens of suppliers. The last two decide whether anything actually changes in your workshop.

01

Capture

Nothing comes in without someone responding.

A garage does not have one channel, it has four. The phone rings, a WhatsApp message comes in, the form on the site sends an email and someone simply walks up to the counter. The first three come together at Helvaro in one stream, with one history per customer.

The point is not that answers come faster. The point is that there always is always an answer. A missed call at a quarter past six is no longer a missed call, but a conversation that started at a quarter past six.

Channels
  • WhatsApp live
  • Website and form live
  • Phone in development
  • Email in development

We deliberately state here what is not there yet. A demo in which something turns out to be missing costs more than an honest list.

02

Understand

The car as well, not just the question.

This is where the difference lies between a general assistant and something that belongs in automotive. "My car is making a strange noise" is, to a chatbot, a sentence. To this system it is a reason to ask for the registration, and from that moment there is a car on the table with a year, a fuel type and an expiry date.

After that the question is no longer "what does this person want", but "is this an MOT, a repair, a service or a price enquiry". That distinction determines which questions follow and how much time the slot should get.

What is on the table
  • Registration and vehicle data
  • Year and fuel
  • MOT expiry date
  • Type of enquiry
  • Customer history, where you make it available

The registration integration is on the RDW page, including what we do and do not store.

03

Act

Answering is the beginning, not the end.

A conversation that ends with "give us a ring" has solved nothing, it has moved the work onto the customer. Helvaro proposes a slot, books it, confirms it and reminds about it. If it stays quiet after a quote, another message follows, and one more, and then it stops.

What does not fit goes through to a person. A damage case, an unhappy customer, a technical discussion about an engine problem. Those go to a named person at your end, with the conversation, so nobody has to start again.

What happens
  • Probing and qualifying
  • Propose a slot and book it
  • Confirm and remind
  • Following up until a yes or no is on the table
  • Handing over with context

What an agent may do independently and what goes past you first, you set per action type. That is under control.

04

Connect

In your existing software, not beside it.

This is the layer where most loose tools fail. An appointment that only sits in a separate little screen has to be retyped by someone. That lasts two weeks and then stops, and the system is quietly abandoned.

Helvaro writes into the planning your team already works in. We only call live what is live. The rest is marked in development or planned, and that only changes once it works at a real garage.

State of play
  • Google Calendar live
  • AutomaaT GO in development
  • RDW registration in development
  • Autoflex planned
  • WinCar planned

All the details per integration are on the integrations page.

05

Measure

What you do not count does not improve.

The question a garage owner asks after three months is not "does the technology work". It is "did it earn me anything". For that you need to know how many conversations came in, how many became appointments, and how many of those would have leaked away without this system.

That last number is the only one that really matters, and it is also the hardest. That is why we start the pilot with one figure instead of a dashboard full of charts.

What gets counted
  • Conversations per channel
  • Appointments that came out of them
  • Follow-up still running
  • Recovered missed calls
  • Everything exportable

Exactly what we measure in the pilot is on the numbers page.

Inbound

Your customer calls. Someone picks up.

No opportunity disappears any more because your team was busy. What comes in gets answered, questioned and scheduled.

Picking up while the workshop carries on

Within a few seconds the caller has someone on the line who understands the question and notes the registration. Your mechanic does not put down his spanner and your front desk finishes its conversation.

Probing like your best front-desk colleague

Which car, what fault, since when, how urgent, do you want to wait or drop off. The same questions you would ask, every time, also at a quarter to six.

Scheduling straight away instead of calling back

Proposing a slot, booking it and confirming it all happens in the same conversation. Calling back is a promise that often falls through, and your customer knows that too.

What does not fit goes to a person

A damage case, an angry customer, a technical discussion. Those go through to a named person at your end, with the whole conversation attached so nobody has to start again.

Evenings and Saturdays count too

Anyone who breaks down at eight in the evening is looking for a garage at that moment. An answer the next morning arrives at the second choice.

Dutch and French without a separate number

The customer's language is recognised and the conversation continues in that language. For anyone in Brussels or near the language border, that saves half a front desk.

Outbound

Waiting for the customer to call is not a plan.

You know which cars are due soon. Helvaro turns that into messages that produce appointments.
